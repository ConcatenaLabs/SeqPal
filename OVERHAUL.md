# SeqPal Radical Overhaul: Final Plan
**Version 2026-07-11. Synthesized from the winning issuer-compliance-first design of a three-design judged panel, with the best ideas of the other designs grafted in, all judge concerns resolved, and all 38 adversarial-verification findings applied. Binding user decisions honored: SeqPal-operated compliance (decision 1), USDX and BTC payments for subscriptions and platform fees (decision 2), P2P secondary in scope with SeqDEX as a handover document only (decision 3), issuer and investor journeys at equal fidelity (decision 4). ## 1. Executive summary

SeqPal today does one real thing: it mints a real restricted asset on the live Sequentia testnet. Everything else, identity, payments, escrow, settlement, the share register, servicing, compliance enforcement, and the legal layer, is browser-side theater held in a single localStorage key. This overhaul turns SeqPal into a working proof of concept in which **no financial fact is ever asserted by a browser**: the chain (the Sequentia network, anchored to Bitcoin testnet4) is the register, the OpenAMP policy server (openampd) is the enforcement arm that refuses ineligible transfers with real 403s, and seqpald grows into a persistent multi-principal backend that operates escrow wallets on both chains, orchestrates atomic delivery-versus-payment, runs a distribution engine, generates content-addressed legal documents bound to the on-chain contract hash, and holds the only privileged openampd credential.

The compliance mechanism becomes real even where identity content stays simulated: a sanctioned test persona is really refused, an ineligible or locked-up resale really fails at the policy server for the life of the asset, expired accreditation really blocks transfers, per-member-state EU holder caps and Reg S distribution-compliance windows are really enforced at co-sign time, and every refusal lands in a public, hash-chained transparency log anchored via OP_RETURN on the Sequentia network, which itself anchors to Bitcoin testnet4. Money moves for real on testnet rails: USDX subscriptions settle as one atomic multi-asset transaction (tokens to the investor's enclave, USDX to the issuer, platform fee to SeqPal, one explorer txid), and BTC subscriptions settle on an honest cross-chain registrar model (payment observed and confirmed on Bitcoin testnet4, delivery co-signed on the Sequentia network), with the trust difference surfaced, refund addresses captured up front, and the reorg failure window handled explicitly. The investor's primary custody path is the ordinary live Sequentia web wallet: delivered securities appear unprompted next to the investor's BTC, embodying the dual-chain first principle. What stays simulated (KYC vendor review, PEP screening, the e-signature provider, Próspera e-registry and RFSA regulator approval, fiat rails, the DR custodian, tax remittance) is labeled on the surface where it appears, and produces real, hashed, anchored artifacts anyway.

Regulatory realism is a first-class deliverable, equal to the technical one: Próspera (RFSA) is the fully legitimate issuance jurisdiction, and every investor-side jurisdiction gets critical treatment: promotion gates with statutory UK investor statements, US 506(c) verified-accreditation with validity windows, per-member-state EU caps with offeree counting at the promotion gate, excluded-by-default treatment of unanalyzed jurisdictions, source-of-funds and appropriateness overlays, real OFAC SDN screening with a manual-review queue, withholding math on distributions, a stated platform-role and custody analysis, and a privacy page that promises exactly what ships and no more.

## 3. Target architecture

### 3.0 What a SeqPal ID is (the identity layer, not an issuer login)

SeqPal ID is the KYC and AML identity layer for the whole OpenAMP-on-Sequentia world, and it sits UPSTREAM of both products. There is one registration flow, identical for everybody, and it never asks whether you are an issuer or an investor: no user-type selector, no fork, role-neutral copy. What registration produces is a verified identity bound to an enclave key (the AID). The platform then stamps eligibility categories onto that AID (M2), and those categories gate everything downstream.

The same single credential opens different doors depending on what the person does next: an issuer signs into the issuance platform with it; an investor needs it to be permitted to hold or trade SeqPal-managed restricted assets AT ALL, including on a Sequentia DEX that carries SeqPal listings (a venue cannot grant eligibility, it can only check what SeqPal stamped, which is exactly the model in the integration spec's venue part). So "issuer" and "investor" are things a verified identity DOES later, never a type chosen at signup, and a corporate (KYB) entity is an addition to an existing personal SeqPal ID, never an alternative signup path.

This is why the identity surface is a compliance passport rather than a product login, and why the investor journey and the issuer journey share one front door.

**SeqPal ID is therefore a first-class standalone surface with its own route and landing (decided 2026-07-11), delivered in M2** when categories become real and the passport finally has something true to show. It is a sub-app under `/id/*`, with its own shell (the IdNav and IdFooter components already exist and already describe themselves as a standalone subsite), reachable without ever touching the issuance platform:

- `/id` LANDING: what a SeqPal ID is and what it unlocks, in role-neutral terms (hold and trade SeqPal-managed restricted assets anywhere on Sequentia, including on a venue that lists them; and, if you ever want to, issue). One call to action: create or sign in. No issuer framing, no user-type question.
- `/id/register`: the single registration flow (key generation, passphrase, mandatory encrypted-backup export, simulated-but-labeled document review, real sanctions screening), identical for everyone.
- `/id/passport`: the credential itself. AID, the enclave key it binds, eligibility categories with their validity windows and expiry warnings, per-list screening status with lastScreenedAt, linked corporate (KYB) entities, and WHERE THE ID IS ACCEPTED: which SeqPal-managed assets the holder is eligible for and which venues honor it. This is the page a regulator or a counterparty would ask to see.
- `/id/entities`: add and manage corporate (KYB) entities as additions to the personal ID.

The marketing site presents SeqPal ID as a PRODUCT in its own right (Products page), not as the issuance platform's login. The issuance platform and the investor portal both consume it.

## 4. Regulatory architecture

Próspera (RFSA) is the issuance jurisdiction, fully legitimate, never questioned. Every investor-side jurisdiction gets critical treatment. Design principle: UI gates are pre-flights; the policy server is the gate; restrictions persist for the life of the asset because every enclave spend requires the policy co-signature.

### 4.1 Category taxonomy (canonical, platform-owned, versioned)

Compound tokens encode AND-semantics inside openampd's any-of matching: `j:<ISO2>:<elig>` with elig in {ret, acc, pro}, for example `j:DE:ret`, `j:US:acc`, `j:GB:hnw`, `j:GB:soph`, `j:HN-PRO:ret` (Próspera residents). The EU is split into member-state codes (fixes item 96). Sanctions status and KYC staleness are NOT categories: a confirmed sanctions hit triggers POST /v1/issuer/freeze (global, hard, correct under a single-operator model); staleness is a category removal by the expiry cron.

**Vocabulary versioning:** the taxonomy carries a version number stored in seqpald. Every user's category list in openampd is a pure projection of the user's seqpald claims record plus the current vocabulary version. Migration procedure when the vocabulary changes (for example splitting a code): (1) bump the version, (2) migrate claims records to new tokens by rule, (3) re-stamp every affected user through the serialized write queue, (4) recompile every affected asset's allowed_categories via POST /v1/issuer/rules, (5) audit-log the migration with before/after hashes. Assets and users are never allowed to reference tokens from two versions simultaneously.

**Rules amendments bind to the document chain.** Every POST /v1/issuer/rules mutation, whatever its origin (vocabulary migration, closing engine, corporate-action console), emits a content-addressed rules-amendment document recording the prior rules hash, the new rules hash, the basis, and the effective height, anchored via the transparency log. The asset page and the "Verify independently" explainer render the chain as "genesis terms_hash plus anchored amendments", so the on-chain contract commitment stays truthful after the first mutation: GET /v1/assets/{id} rules must always equal the head of the anchored amendment chain (this equality is an M7 acceptance proof), and holder notices go out through the portal inbox for material changes.

**US-person capture.** The Reg S "U.S. person" definition (17 CFR 230.902(k)) is broader than a residence code: it reaches entities organized under US law, estates and trusts with US fiduciaries, and certain agency accounts, and US tax status turns on citizenship as well as residence. The verification questionnaire therefore captures US-person status as an explicit determination (natural persons: residence AND citizenship or green-card status for the tax leg; entities, if ever admitted: place of organization and fiduciary status), stored in the claims record and feeding the j:US-prefixed tokens and the W-9/W-8 election independently of the residence field. The taxonomy documentation notes the divergence.

**Write concurrency:** openampd category writes are replace-whole-list. seqpald therefore routes ALL category writes (verification flow, expiry cron, sanctions path, vocabulary migration) through one per-user serialized write queue: read the projection under a per-user mutex, compute the full new list from the claims record, write, verify by re-reading GET /v1/users/{aid}, audit-log pre/post lists and vocabulary version. Three writers, one door.

**Cross-asset expiry semantics, stated intent:** categories are platform-wide identity attributes by design. Expiry of `j:US:acc` strips eligibility for every asset the holder owns, which is intended: stale accreditation is stale everywhere. Mitigations: the cron notifies the holder 14 days before expiry (portal notice), and any subscription in flight at expiry is re-checked at settlement time and fails closed into the refund path rather than settling against a stale category.

### 4.2 Jurisdictional access model: view, subscribe, hold

**Instrument characterization comes first.** The Equity SPV and Debt/Yield structures may constitute AIFs (EU, AIFMD) or unregulated collective investment schemes (UK, FSMA s.235), in which case prospectus-exemption counting and FPO statements are the wrong frameworks for those products: EU marketing of an AIF runs through Article 42 NPPR registration per member state (largely professional-only), and UK promotion runs through FSMA s.238 and the Promotion of CISs (Exemptions) Order rather than FPO Articles 48/50A. The M4 legal pipeline therefore generates a per-structure instrument-characterization memo (structure, security vs AIF/CIS analysis with the AIFMD holding-company and single-asset considerations stated, applicable marketing regime per jurisdiction, PRIIPs applicability), and the Step 5 matrix compiler CONSUMES the classification: an AIF-classified structure disables the EU retail lift, restricts EU access to professional categories consistent with Art 42 NPPR, and switches the UK gate to the CIS-promotion exemption wording. The classification surfaces on the Legal & Licensing page and in the generated risk factors.

**View (financial promotion, item 95).** Offering pages are served by seqpald and filtered on a `promotable_jurisdictions` facet of the Step 5 matrix (part of the terms object, hence committed in terms_hash). Anonymous visitors get a teaser and a gate. The teaser is a fixed template, not issuer free text: issuer name, sector, structure type, jurisdiction-availability statement, and a neutral "verification required to view offering details" call to action; no price, no raise size, no returns language, no subscription mechanics, so the ungated page stays below the EU "offer of securities to the public" line (PR Art 2(d)) and the UK s.21 inducement line. Teaser content is template-generated and covered by the copy grep gates. UK residents must record the statutory high-net-worth or self-certified sophisticated investor statement before the offering renders, using the SI 2024/301 wording and thresholds (GBP 100k income / GBP 250k net assets), carried verbatim into the generated statement artifact; statements are valid 12 months and recorded on the ID. Restricted jurisdictions require a qualified-investor attestation. **Offeree counting:** for EU member states relying on the sub-150 exemption, seqpald counts distinct non-qualified persons per member state to whom the offer is made available (gate passage), warns the issuer approaching 149, and blocks the 150th offeree at the gate. The on-chain per-category HOLDER cap of 149 is retained as a deliberate, documented over-enforcement: Prospectus Regulation Art 1(4)(b) counts offerees, not holders, so refusing the 150th secondary transferee is a conservative approximation, stated as such in the generated offering documents and the Legal & Licensing page.

**Subscribe.** Server-side preflight: openampd categories vs asset rules, plus per-offering overlays where retail was lift-admitted: appropriateness questionnaire and a generated KID-style summary with a standardized risk banner (item 107; the artifact itself and the lift basis record carry the label "KID-style summary; production offerings of SPV or DR structures to EU retail require a PRIIPs-compliant KID", derived from the characterization memo); a source-of-funds questionnaire above USD 10k-equivalent (item 108). US access is 506(c) only: `j:US:acc` requires an uploaded verification artifact (content simulated, hash + validity window real, 90-day style staleness), accredited defaults to FALSE (item 94). All of these gates ship IN the subscription milestone (M5): no milestone ever demos real money moving without the offer-side gates.

**Hold and secondary (items 91, 96, 105, 106).** The Step 5 matrix compiles at deploy into openampd rules: standard jurisdiction admits `j:XX:ret|acc|pro`; restricted admits `acc|pro` only; excluded/blocked admits nothing; the catch-all is EXCLUDED BY DEFAULT (flips item 105); lockup selector maps to `lockin_until_height`; Reg S distribution-compliance periods map to the new `category_denies: [{prefix:"j:US", until_height}]` (OA-3), with the generated documents carrying 17 CFR 230.903 distribution-compliance language; EU per-member-state caps map to the new `holder_caps_by_category` (OA-3); structure defaults set `velocity_*`. The restriction legend (summary + terms_hash) is embedded in the contract JSON, visible at GET /v1/assets/{id} and in wallets. These persist on every secondary transfer automatically.

**Primary-settlement scoping (the rule that keeps the platform from refusing its own closing).** Deploy-time lockups and Reg S category denies must not block the primary distribution itself: openampd's checkTransfer as it stands would refuse ANY non-issuer-bound transfer during lockin_until_height, including delivery from the per-offering escrow enclave to investors at close. The OA-3 rules spec therefore includes `rules.primary_aids`, listing the per-offering escrow and entity treasury AIDs: `allowed_categories` still applies to every recipient on primary sends, but `lockin_until_height` and `category_denies` bind only transfers whose sender is NOT a primary AID. The M2 acceptance proof includes both directions: escrow-to-investor delivery succeeds during the lockup window while an investor-to-investor transfer in the same window is refused.

**US resale restrictions attach to the seller, not just the recipient (Rule 144).** Tokens bought by US investors under 506(c) are restricted securities: resale requires Rule 144 (12-month holding period for a non-reporting issuer), Section 4(a)(7) (accredited purchaser plus information conditions), or offshore resale under Rule 904. A recipient-category model cannot express a seller-side restriction, so the closing engine stamps each US-tranche purchaser's AID with a per-AID vesting entry (openampd's existing rules.Vesting primitive: until-height, atoms) covering their full allocation until close height plus approximately 12 months, giving a real, co-sign-enforced Rule 144 approximation. The offering memorandum and the restriction legend state the three resale pathways, and the residual divergence (the 4(a)(7) information conditions and Rule 904 offshore mechanics are platform-layer records, not co-sign-enforced) is documented on the Legal & Licensing page and in the integration spec's venue regulatory notes (Part 2.7).

**Concurrent Reg S and 506(c) tranches are distinct offerings.** The generated offering documents state the structure explicitly: offshore purchasers are bound by the 17 CFR 230.903(b) distribution-compliance period; US 506(c) purchasers hold Rule 144 restricted securities and are NOT part of the Reg S tranche; the two offerings are not integrated (Rule 152 framework).

**Lockup honesty:** lockups are enforced at a Sequentia block height. Investor-facing copy and the generated documents state the lockup as "until Sequentia block H (approximately N months assuming the documented block cadence; block times on this testnet vary and the height, not the date, is binding)".

### 4.3 Secondary-market regulatory surface

Beyond category enforcement: (a) travel-rule-style counterparty capture: every P2P transfer record in seqpald stores originator and beneficiary identity (both AIDs resolve to registered platform identities; wallet-initiated transfers are captured by the /v1/log poller and joined to identities server-side); (b) a market-abuse and insider-dealing acknowledgment is recorded once per investor before the platform's transfer surfaces are enabled, disclosed as a platform-layer control (not policy-enforced at co-sign, stated honestly); (c) ongoing disclosure: the servicing engine generates a labeled-simulated annual report to holders, delivered to the portal inbox and hash-anchored. All three are also noted in the integration spec's venue part (openamp repo, spec/venue-wallet-integration.md, Part 2.7).

### 4.4 Documents bound to terms_hash (items 26, 32, 60, 80, 93, 99)

seqpald renders deterministic offering documents (offering memorandum with per-structure risk factors, subscription agreement, operating agreement declaring the on-chain register dispositive under Próspera law, escrow terms, KID-style summary, UK statutory statements, lift artifacts) to content-addressed artifacts (canonical HTML plus PDF via the house soffice toolchain). A sha256 manifest of the package goes INSIDE the terms object; seqpald canonicalizes and hashes terms server-side (rejects non-64-hex), stores the canonical document, serves GET /seqpal/api/terms/{hash}. **Document access is gated during the offer window:** the hash manifest is public immediately, but document preimages are served only to identities that have passed the promotion gate (gate passage is already the offeree-counting event, so the gate and the count stay consistent); preimages publish ungated automatically at offer close, and holders and signers always retain access. Without this, anyone worldwide holding the hash (which is embedded in the public on-chain contract and registry entry) could fetch the complete offering package mid-offer, defeating the UK reasonable-belief conditions, the EU offeree-counting model, and Reg S directed-selling-efforts discipline. A standing probe gate asserts that no offer-period document endpoint returns 200 to an anonymous request. E-signature = BIP340 signature by the signer's enclave key over the document hash (wallet-linked users sign via the wallet's sign-request card), stored and anchored via the transparency log; the e-sign provider remains a labeled simulation. A public "Verify independently" explainer walks an auditor from the terms document to terms_hash to the on-chain contract_hash to the policy-key commitment, beyond bare explorer links; it states the access model plainly (hash commitment verifiable immediately, preimage public at offer close) and renders the anchored rules-amendment chain (Section 4.1) alongside the genesis commitment.

### 4.6 Sanctions and KYC (items 2, 7, 34, 72, 90)

Real: seqpald screens registered names against three public sources at ID creation and daily by cron: the OFAC SDN plus OFAC consolidated non-SDN lists, the EU consolidated financial sanctions list, and the UN Security Council consolidated list (all public downloads; a platform admitting EU and UK personas cannot screen US lists alone). Each review-queue item records the originating list and matched entry, and the Passport shows per-list lastScreenedAt. **Manual-review queue:** a screening hit does not auto-refuse; it moves the ID to "pending review" with a seqpald admin review surface; a confirmed hit refuses the ID or freezes the AID (logged); a cleared false positive proceeds. The reviewer surface is a minimal session-gated admin page on seqpald, plus a labeled SIMULATED auto-reviewer that confirms or clears the deterministic TEST personas (test identities inside the KYC-simulation boundary; nothing else on the platform is scripted) after a short delay, so walkthroughs run unattended; a human can always act first from the admin page. Simulated (labeled): document/selfie review (server-side queue with pending/approved/rejected/needs-info states and refusal personas), PEP/adverse-media. The artifact is real either way: a seqpald-signed claims record {aid, residence, eligibility, verifiedAt, expiry} drives category assignment.

### 4.7 Tax and reporting (items 61, 101)

Each ID carries a simulated-form, real-math tax status (W-9 vs W-8BEN with treaty rate). The distribution engine computes gross/withheld/net per holder, pays NET on-chain, generates 1042-S-style annual artifacts and an issuer withholding summary; DR distributions show an explicit dividend-equivalent withholding line. Remittance to authorities is out of scope and labeled.

FATCA/CRS: a production registrar and transfer agent holding investor accounts is plausibly a Financial Institution under CRS, with self-certification and annual reporting duties for foreign account holders, and a W-8/W-9 is not a CRS self-certification. Investor onboarding therefore includes a CRS/FATCA self-certification step (all tax residencies plus TINs, stored in the claims record), the servicing engine emits a labeled-simulated annual CRS/FATCA reporting artifact from the registrar entity alongside the 1042-S-style artifacts, and the Legal & Licensing page notes the registrar's likely FI classification.

### 4.8 Data protection (items 15, 18, 109)

Privacy page ships with promises scoped to exactly what ships at that moment, and is updated when log minimization lands (M8). It states plainly: **erasure is partial.** Server-side identity records are deletable; the chain and the anchored transparency log retain pseudonymous AIDs and opaque hashes that cannot be deleted; an AID with balance history linked to a server-side identity record is arguably personal data, and the strongest available remedy on erasure is destruction of the AID-to-identity mapping plus optional AID rotation (clawback and re-delivery to a fresh AID), documented as a procedure. Transparency-log minimization (category events log the hash of the category set, not the raw list) is implemented in-plan (M8, OA-LM). Self-hosted fonts and a strict CSP land in M1.

Extraterritoriality: actively targeting EU investors triggers GDPR Article 3(2), so the privacy page carries an applicability statement, a lawful-basis table per processing purpose (identity and screening under compliance-with-legal-obligation and legitimate-interest bases, with the transparency log's publication basis stated explicitly), a labeled-simulated Article 27 EU representative entry consistent with the simulated-registrations posture, and an international-transfer note (SCCs as the production design for EU investor data processed on the Próspera-side platform).

Public wallet-surface exposure, disclosed: GET /v1/users/{aid} publicly returns each AID's category tokens (identity-adjacent once they encode jurisdiction and eligibility, for example j:US:acc) and frozen status, and balances are publicly queryable per AID; OA-LM minimizes only transparency-log entries, not this read surface. The privacy page discloses it, and the SeqDEX eligibility preflight explicitly depends on it staying readable; hashing category tokens with a resolution endpoint for authorized venues is noted as a possible follow-up, out of scope here.

## 8. Internal acceptance checklist (engineering QA; never given to testers)

This checklist is an ENGINEERING artifact: the sequence of real operations we perform live to verify every capability works end to end before calling the overhaul done. It is never handed to testers or users, and no in-product tour or guided script replaces it: real issuers and investors get only the product's own UI, so the PoC must be navigable unaided. If a tester needs this document to find the next step, that is a usability DEFECT to fix, not a documentation gap to fill. Every step below is something any user could do ad lib; the only simulated elements encountered are exactly the Section 7 list (KYC/KYB review content, sanctions vendor relationship, e-signature provider, incorporation/regulator, fiat rails, DR custodian, tax forms), each labeled where it appears. The walkthrough prescribes ACTIONS, never economics: raise size, price per token, minimum subscription, and every other deal term are set by the person playing the issuer at structuring time, exactly as on the real platform, and the platform imposes no demo caps. Where a number appears below it is only an illustration of what that issuer might type in that run. Raises up to roughly USD 1M per investor are fundable with real flows because the M5 faucet-capacity workstream re-issues USDX (and the other reissuable assets) and raises the faucet accordingly. The offering price the issuer chose seeds the ticker's /prices entry at deploy.

1. **Issuer onboarding (browser A).** Founder creates an individual SeqPal ID: real key, real SDN screen stamp, simulated document review with a visible pending state. Adds the corporate entity: KYB queue, the entity gets its own enclave key and treasury AID, UBO-signed link. 2. **Structuring.** The issuer picks an Equity SPV and enters their own deal terms (for illustration: 1,000,000 tokens at USD 1.00); jurisdiction matrix: Próspera retail, DE retail via lift (uploaded basis artifact), US 506(c) accredited-only, catch-all excluded; a lockup chosen by the issuer, shown as "until Sequentia block H"; clawback on (issuer's toggle).

5. **Investor A (DE retail, browser B), wallet-first.** Opens the ordinary live web wallet, links its AID to a new SeqPal ID by signing a challenge in the wallet's sign-request card. Passes the promotion gate (DE lift basis shown), appropriateness questionnaire, reads the KID and risk factors, signs the subscription agreement hash via the wallet card, funds USDX from the faucet helper into the segregated escrow deposit address, watches confirmations tick.
6. **Investor B (US accredited).** Uploads the verification artifact, category granted with a visible expiry; chooses to pay in **BTC on Bitcoin testnet4** from the wallet's BTC lane (rail choice is always the investor's; this run exercises the BTC rail here), refund address captured at subscription; the UI shows the trust comparison: "USDX: atomic delivery-versus-payment, one transaction" vs "BTC: cross-chain registrar settlement, payment confirmed on Bitcoin testnet4, then delivery co-signed on the Sequentia network; not atomic". 7. **Closing.** The issuer signs the closing authorization once. Investor A's settlement is **one atomic txid** on the explorer: tokens to her enclave, USDX to the issuer's registered payout address, the platform fee output to SeqPal. Investor B's tokens are delivered by policy-co-signed transfer after his testnet4 confirmations. 9. **Servicing.** The issuer runs a USDX dividend: record-date snapshot, pro-rata, investor B's line shows real withholding math, NET amounts paid on-chain to registered payout mandate addresses with per-holder txids and downloadable statements.
